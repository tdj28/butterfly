# FND-037 — The left unstable lobe enters the three-branch saddle

Status: qualified retrospective geometry from passed EXP-145

## Finding

The UPO unstable atlas reaches the declared left lobe on both sides of the
local transition. The topology change is therefore not the creation of that
lobe. At `a=0.148`, however, none of 4200 post-burn-in PIM states across three
access lines and two independently qualified horizons lies in the lobe. At
`a=0.14825`, every access line and both horizons contain 11–15 such states.

The three-side PIM lobe is geometrically tied to the UPO unstable atlas in the
full Barrio section. Every retained PIM lobe state lies within scaled distance
`2.399e-5` of the nine-amplitude atlas and `6.099e-5` of its nested
five-amplitude subset.

## Implication for Jones

This is good news for the core mechanism. Together with persistence of all
eleven recovered UPO families, it supports the interpretation that a
pre-existing unstable lobe changes its membership in the nonattracting chaotic
saddle—consistent with a pruning/reinjection event—rather than appearing in a
local orbit bifurcation.

It does not yet prove Jones's stronger rotation, spiral organization, or
parameter-plane claims. EXP-145 is retrospective, PIM clouds approximate the
invariant saddle, and nearest-neighbor overlap is not an exact transverse or
tangent manifold intersection. A fresh parameter must prospectively show that
branch class and lobe inclusion agree before this residual becomes
claim-bearing. EXP-149 subsequently supplies that held-out pass at
`a=0.1481875`: its blind two-branch PIM saddle excludes the same frozen lobe on
all six access-line/horizon reconstructions; see FND-043.
