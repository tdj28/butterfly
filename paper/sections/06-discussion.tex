\section{Discussion and limitations}

The reconstructed computations strengthen Jones's central research direction
without retroactively validating every 2012 claim.  Exact orbit continuation
now reaches much deeper than the original finite-time period plot, exposes
double-cover and conditioning failure modes, and makes successful prospective
event predictions.  The section-grazing result also supplies a concrete reason
to distinguish an invariant flow bifurcation from the topology of a chosen
measurement.

The qualified three-branch control is good news for the existence of the local
object used by Jones, but its orientation dependence sharpens rather than
removes the central limitation.  Robust agreement between $x$ and $z$ on the
historical half-plane shows that the result is not a single-coordinate
accident.  Failure of a stable scalar graph on the opposite half-plane shows
why scalar branch counts must be embedded in the two-dimensional Poincar\'e
map before they are assigned topological or reinjection meaning.

The attracting-set transition band is progress toward a curve, not a surrogate
for the curve.  In particular, the published bimodal chaotic saddle at
$a=0.149$ coexists with a stable period-4 attractor, whereas the first robustly
three-branch attracting sample in the frozen path occurs at $a=0.160$.
Resolving that distinction requires computing the nonattracting invariant set;
interpolating attractor labels across the regular window would erase the main
advance of the Barrio analysis.

The results do not yet explain every shrimp or hub in the full $(a,c)$ plane.
They establish one local cascade at fixed $(a,c)$ and a set of computational
methods that can be extended.  A plane-wide explanation requires continued
surfaces for periodic-orbit bifurcations, topology changes, equilibria, Hopf and
homoclinic structure, plus tests of which structures actually predict window
connectivity.  Raster morphology alone cannot supply that explanation.

The corrected historical position is also narrower.  Earlier work established
spiral organization and the topology-transition line
\citep{barrio2011global}; finite logistic-like ordering in flows was already
known \citep{lefranc1994combining}; and genuinely two-dimensional maps obstruct
a global periodic-orbit conjugacy \citep{holmes1984bifurcation}.  The remaining
distinctive Jones hypothesis is not generic logistic ordering.  It is whether
a precisely defined third-branch reinjection observable explains particular
symbolic $p\mathbin{\to}p+1$ connections within the broader hub skeleton.

Generality beyond R{\"o}ssler is deliberately deferred.  Only after the branch
oracle, finite symbolic test, and reinjection observable pass prospectively on
R{\"o}ssler will their definitions be frozen and applied unchanged to unlike
flows.  Negative cross-system results will constrain, rather than invalidate,
the local R{\"o}ssler findings.
