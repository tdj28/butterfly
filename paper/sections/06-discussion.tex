\section{Discussion and limitations}

The reconstructed computations strengthen Jones's central research direction
without retroactively validating every 2012 claim.  Exact orbit continuation
now reaches much deeper than the original finite-time period plot, exposes
double-cover and conditioning failure modes, and makes successful prospective
event predictions.  The section-grazing result also supplies a concrete reason
to distinguish an invariant flow bifurcation from the topology of a chosen
measurement.

The qualified three-branch control is good news for the existence of the local
object used by Jones, but its orientation dependence sharpens rather than
removes the central limitation.  Robust agreement between $x$ and $z$ on the
historical half-plane shows that the result is not a single-coordinate
accident.  Failure of a stable scalar graph on the opposite half-plane shows
why scalar branch counts must be embedded in the two-dimensional Poincar\'e
map before they are assigned topological or reinjection meaning.

The attracting-set transition band is progress toward a curve, not a surrogate
for the curve.  In particular, the published bimodal chaotic saddle at
$a=0.149$ coexists with a stable period-4 attractor, whereas the first robustly
three-branch attracting sample in the frozen path occurs at $a=0.160$.
Resolving that distinction requires computing the nonattracting invariant set;
interpolating attractor labels across the regular window would erase the main
advance of the Barrio analysis.

The first saddle ensemble is encouraging but deliberately not promoted to a
reproduction.  It contains the shallow additional extremum at $a=0.149$, while
the preregistered global prominence floor removes it.  This is not merely a
tuning inconvenience: a branch-opening bifurcation begins at zero prominence,
so a global amplitude threshold systematically shifts its detected location.
The replacement test must attach uncertainty to the local derivative reversal
and converge ensemble statistics across numerical resolutions.  That
replacement now retains the expected two/three distinction in all 300 topology
cells, including step halving and later conditioning.  Its remaining failure
is concentrated in lattice-sampled survivor density and one linearly
interpolated event time.  The evidence for the local saddle shape is therefore
substantially stronger than the evidence for a converged lifetime measure; the
two claims must remain separate.

The scrambled-Sobol and cubic-event successor closes both remaining numerical
gates and qualifies the two published saddle controls.  This is direct support
for the shared 2012 premise that the branch distinction persists when the
chaotic set is nonattracting inside a regular window.  For Jones specifically,
it validates a necessary substrate for third-branch reinjection, not the claim
that reinjection predicts the spiral connections.  That explanatory step still
requires a measurable observable and held-out predictions.

The GPU reproduction adds a backend check and a practical scaling path without
changing that scientific boundary.  Agreement of survivor distributions,
critical locations, and branch labels shows that the local result is not tied
to the CPU implementation.  It does not provide the structurally independent
orbit restraint supplied by PIM triples or stagger-and-step, so that
corroboration remains mandatory before interpreting a continued saddle-defined
boundary.

The first PIM experiment partly supplies that corroboration.  Three adaptive
straddles independently recover the bimodal branch geometry and agree with the
sprinkler's critical locations, but the experiment fails because its zero-
censor rule rejects every unimodal line and two bimodal lines.  This does not
weaken the sprinkler result: survival beyond the censor horizon is positive
evidence for proximity to the stable set, although it does not by itself order
neighboring candidates.  A prospectively defined right-censored PIM rule and
nested lifetime horizons are required before the unimodal control can close.

The censor-aware successor closes the fixed-128-return two-control comparison,
but deliberately fails its stronger nested-horizon gate.  At the longer
horizon, independently restrained trajectories recover the two- and three-
branch maps in both coordinates and agree with the sprinkler's critical
locations.  At 64 returns, the unimodal trajectories sample too little of the
invariant domain and the bimodal construction resolves too few access lines.
Thus the lifetime ceiling controls which stable-set filaments and invariant
subset the PIM procedure covers, not merely its runtime.  The result strengthens
the local branch substrate relevant to Jones while requiring a longer-horizon
comparison before PIM-based boundary continuation.

That longer-horizon comparison now passes.  Doubling the adequate 128-return
ceiling to 256 preserves all six access-line straddles, both branch counts, and
critical locations in both coordinates while nearly eliminating censored
lifetime evaluations.  Together with the independent sprinkler result, this
closes the planned finite-horizon method-corroboration gate at the two controls.
It does not prove infinite-time convergence or template equivalence; it does
justify moving from repeated control reproduction to prospective saddle-
defined continuation through regular parameter gaps.

The first held-out continuation is informative precisely because it does not
pass wholesale.  A new regular-window point at $a=0.140$ qualifies as
two-branch and narrows the saddle bracket, whereas $a=0.145$ loses invariant-
domain support before the strict oracle can decide.  More importantly, the
qualified three-branch saddle at $a=0.149$ is adjacent to an earlier nominal
two-branch $a=0.150$ candidate.  The audit finds that candidate to be persistent
chaos, but its shallow extremum is suppressed only by the coarsest binning;
every resolved 30--80-bin variant sees three branches.  This removes the basis
for claiming an immediate topology reversal.  The prospective successor then
passes on new doubled-length data, reproducing the coarse miss and qualifying
the three-branch label at adequate resolution.  Local continuity across
$a=0.149/0.150$ is supported; the unresolved $a=0.145$ saddle now limits the
boundary bracket.  Increasing its sprinkler support by a factor of eight
removes the finite-sample failure: every resolved cell is two-branch, and the
remaining failures occur only in the 80-bin $z$ projections because equal-width
bins cover locations outside the projected invariant support.  Additional
trajectories cannot populate such locations.  Treating these failures as
censored rather than contradictory is plausible, and the prospective
successor now qualifies that treatment: both controls resolve normally, while
all new $a=0.145$ variants are either resolved two-branch observations or
admissible coverage censors with matching critical geometry.  The sampled
saddle bracket therefore narrows to $[0.145,0.149]$.  This supports continuation
through the regular window but does not interpolate a continuous curve between
the samples.  A blind midpoint strengthens the local ordering: all ensembles
and coordinates select two branches at $a=0.147$, shrinking the interval to
$[0.147,0.149]$.  Repeated bisection can localize a finite classifier boundary,
but an independent continuation construction is still needed to establish a
continuous dynamical curve.  The failed $a=0.148$ midpoint makes that warning
concrete.  The extra branch appears across independent 300-unit survivor
subsets but disappears when survival is conditioned through 360 time units.
Because the sprinkler retains midpoint crossings only from final survivors,
this is evidence of escape-lifetime selection rather than a qualified topology crossing.  Horizon
convergence is therefore part of the invariant-set definition near the
boundary, not merely a runtime sensitivity check.

The nested-horizon result sharpens that interpretation.  Every resolved
360--480-unit cell is two-branch, including two independent scrambles and a
doubled sample, while no cell resolves as three.  The only failures occur at
80 bins when the final survivor population is smallest.  Bootstrap instability
cannot be counted as agreement.  A PIM construction that targets
the saddle without exponentially discarding initial conditions is therefore
the independent discriminator.  That blind construction now passes at two
censor ceilings: all six access straddles complete, both coordinates select
two across all oracle variants, and critical locations agree.  The result
narrows the finite classifier bracket to $[0.148,0.149]$.  At the next blind midpoint, the
identical PIM definition instead resolves three branches at both horizons,
localizing the finite boundary to $[0.148,0.1485]$.  The next blind midpoint at
$a=0.14825$ also resolves three at both horizons, halving the bracket to
$[0.148,0.14825]$.  It does not by itself prove an
infinite-lifetime saddle or continue a codimension-one TBA curve.

The prospective lifetime test resolves the remaining mechanistic ambiguity in
an unexpected direction.  The $a=0.148$ extra branch does not capture faster
in restricted mean lifetime: it persists about 30 units longer than the pooled
core on three independent ensembles.  Its survival curve is nevertheless
bounded---all assigned trajectories survive 60 residual units, but none
survives 180---whereas the core mixes immediate capture with a rare tail beyond
270.  This crossing-survival pattern explains the 300/360 conditioning split
without making the transient domain part of the PIM saddle.  It rejects a
modern provisional mechanism, not the Jones/Barrio transition, and requires
future reinjection tests to follow the genuine three-branch saddle from the
upper side of the bracket.

The signed-edge successor supplies a first prospective bridge from integer
classification toward continuation.  At $a=0.148125$, every lower-support
derivative remains negative and predicts the independently counted two-branch
class at both horizons and in both coordinates.  This directly operationalizes
Jones's statement that the second-branch edge becomes the added critical point
at the transition.  The adjacent three-branch support nevertheless expands
sharply, so the present data do not justify assuming a smooth zero crossing.
A transverse parameter-space pilot now rejects the simple continuation picture.
The GPU finite-sprinkler method passes away from the narrow boundary but becomes
oracle-dependent at the PIM-local controls.  Its selected adaptive-PIM test
then prospectively falsifies a three/positive prediction at
$(c,a)=(19.8,0.148)$ while fully passing the three/positive endpoint at
$(19.9,0.150)$.  This supports a bending or displacement of the transition as
$c$ decreases, possibly complicated by the neighboring regular-window
interruption, rather than a nearly vertical extrapolation from $c=20$.  A
higher-horizon finite bracket is still not a continuous curve.  The next
mechanistic test must distinguish smooth edge criticality from an abrupt
branch-opening or pruning event and identify the unstable periodic orbit or
manifold collision involved.

The recovered periodic skeleton now narrows that mechanism test.  Eleven
identity-qualified primitive UPO families---including two independently
distinct lag-12 families---all persist across the complete local bracket and
retain their fundamental Barrio-section crossing counts.  Hence creation,
destruction, family conversion, or crossing loss of any recovered orbit cannot
explain the sampled two/three-branch change.  This does not exclude an
unrecovered orbit bifurcation, but it makes invariant-manifold connectivity,
symbolic pruning, or reinjection the leading tested mechanism class.  The next
calculation must seed phase-resolved stable and unstable manifolds from this
orbit library and define a convergent connection residual; orbit persistence
alone is not a manifold result.

The first seeding stage passes, but its exploratory capture proxy does not.
Directions transported between phases reproduce the signed Floquet
multipliers.  All four atlas-selected capture contrasts nevertheless fail
prospective phase, density, or effect-size gates.  This does not test Jones's
reinjection hypothesis directly---finite-horizon capture was not the claimed
invariant---but it prevents attractor-capture timing from being substituted
for a manifold result.  A direct intersection, turnstile, or symbolic-pruning
residual is now required.

The results do not yet explain every shrimp or hub in the full $(a,c)$ plane.
They establish one local cascade at fixed $(a,c)$ and a set of computational
methods that can be extended.  A plane-wide explanation requires continued
surfaces for periodic-orbit bifurcations, topology changes, equilibria, Hopf and
homoclinic structure, plus tests of which structures actually predict window
connectivity.  Raster morphology alone cannot supply that explanation.

The corrected historical position is also narrower.  Earlier work established
spiral organization and the topology-transition line
\citep{barrio2011global}; finite logistic-like ordering in flows was already
known \citep{lefranc1994combining}; and genuinely two-dimensional maps obstruct
a global periodic-orbit conjugacy \citep{holmes1984bifurcation}.  The remaining
distinctive Jones hypothesis is not generic logistic ordering.  It is whether
a precisely defined third-branch reinjection observable explains particular
symbolic $p\mathbin{\to}p+1$ connections within the broader hub skeleton.

Generality beyond R{\"o}ssler is deliberately deferred.  Only after the branch
oracle, finite symbolic test, and reinjection observable pass prospectively on
R{\"o}ssler will their definitions be frozen and applied unchanged to unlike
flows.  Negative cross-system results will constrain, rather than invalidate,
the local R{\"o}ssler findings.
