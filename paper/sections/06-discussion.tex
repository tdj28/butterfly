\section{What this changes for the original claims}
\label{sec:claims}

The most important unresolved claim is the step from reinjection geometry
to a predicted symbolic connection between windows. The local alphabet and
several geometric ingredients have support, but the critical words and the
arrows in Figure~\ref{fig:symbolic-chain} have not been independently
reproduced. This is the central evidential gap, not a peripheral limitation
of an otherwise completed explanation.

The results support pursuing Jones's mechanism: periodicity hubs have
reproducible local orbit and return-map structure, and nonattracting chaotic
sets matter inside periodic windows. But generic period doubling does not
establish the proposed extra return, and a nearby homoclinic candidate does
not by itself explain the word ordering or locate the center of the spiral.

Several corrections concern the modern reconstruction. A nominal
period-1536 daughter disappears under stronger orbit correction because it
is a doubled parent. A supposed second flip boundary is the same known
boundary sampled on another path; interpolation created the apparent gap.
And a search based on zero full-flow multipliers used an inappropriate
scalar-map analogy. Keeping these corrections explicit is necessary to
understand which later results remain supported.

The priority is a bounded, independently checked window-to-window test:
locate a critical center without the desired word, encode its corrected
orbit, and follow a proposed $p\to p+1$ connection while measuring the
extra return. A failed link must remain visible alongside successes.
Extending that test to the finite source graph would turn an attributed
construction into a tested explanation. Neither a complete atlas nor
completion of every homoclinic refinement is a prerequisite for beginning
this local symbolic test; claims about the hub's global organizer still
require the corresponding homoclinic and manifold evidence.

The narrowest numerical claims deserve the strongest independent checks.
The eighth returning-arm birth has a stable parent and an unstable
period-3072 candidate on the same sampled side. That supports a subcritical
interpretation, but the child's multiplier near 19 is far from its expected
$+1$ limit at a local doubling. Its branch should be followed toward
vanishing amplitude before local criticality is treated as settled.
Similarly, the apparent homoclinic turn needs parameter-sensitive error
control rather than additional points in the same formulation. The
incomplete radius--tolerance grid shows why simply tightening a solver
tolerance is not enough: rounding-sensitive mesh refinement must first
be understood and controlled.

Generality is a separate question. The current evidence concerns R{\"o}ssler;
a reusable solver is not a successful replication on another flow.
After the local mechanism tests pass, two structurally different systems
should be studied with the same declared definitions, including negative
results. That is the necessary step before a survey of dozens of attractors.
