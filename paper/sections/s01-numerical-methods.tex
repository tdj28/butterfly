This supplement retains the detailed experiment sequence, including failed
controls and interpretations that were revised later.  Statements such as
``next'' or ``at this stage'' refer to that sequence; the main article and
its claim summary state the current conclusions.


Throughout the experiment record, a \emph{corrected} orbit or event denotes a
shooting or augmented-system solution at a specified parameter, rather than an
interpolated raster estimate. Some frozen experiment or file labels retain the
legacy word ``exact''; those identifiers do not assert mathematical exactness.
These are numerical solutions with measured residuals. High-precision
arithmetic does not supply a rigorous enclosure of a continuous-flow solution.
Results for a fixed-step Runge--Kutta map additionally require discretization
convergence before they support a statement about the flow.

Finite-time $(a,c)$ rasters are used for discovery, not for declaring invariant
bifurcations.  Candidate periodic states are corrected by shooting equations
with an explicit phase condition.  Stability is computed from the variational
flow and nontrivial Floquet multipliers.  Every continuation correction is
checked against the intended minimal-period orbit identity so that a
double-covered parent cannot be mislabeled as a doubled child.  Adaptive
DOP853 or Radau integrates the state and variational equations; Newton-style
least squares acts only on the finite-dimensional shooting, phase, parameter,
and pseudo-arclength residuals.  Thus correction is not used as a time
integrator.  For continuation in $c$, the analytic sensitivity forcing is
$f_c=(0,0,-z)$ and the resulting shooting Jacobian is checked against centered
finite differences.

At high period, full-period shooting becomes poorly conditioned and can lose
the flip direction.  We therefore divide an orbit into multiple shooting
segments, enforce continuity between segment endpoints, and obtain the
monodromy action from the product of the segment variational maps.  Child
identity is tested at a common parameter by phase-aligning the entire orbit and
comparing against the double-covered parent, rather than by comparing one
section point.

Numerical tolerances, frozen predictors, failed acceptance gates, hashes, and
output paths are recorded in experiment receipts in the accompanying
repository.  Event predictions used as prospective tests are frozen before the
subsequent crossing scan and refinement.  This separation prevents an
after-the-fact fit from being reported as prediction.

The scalar return-projection work uses a gated classifier. It requires
sampled-domain coverage, a bounded conditional-spread ratio, prominent
spline critical points, and deterministic bootstrap consensus; a failed gate
returns ``unresolved'' rather than a forced branch label.  Each experiment
freezes $\Sigma$, the crossing orientation, scalar coordinate, critical-point
criterion, and perturbation matrix before execution.  A scalar classification
that survives these gates is still representation-bounded: the full
two-dimensional return map must be retained for any topological-invariance or
reinjection claim.

Persistent critical identity is evaluated separately from global branch
count.  A physical critical location predicted from independently resolved
neighboring rows is selected locally in each spline variant, bootstrapped
without requiring the shallow added extremum to be detected, and checked in
both scalar coordinates.  Where attracting support is insufficient, a
middle-time survivor cloud may vote only after factor-two RK4 step-size parity,
complete attractor-capture controls, and short-horizon adaptive-DOP853
state/event agreement.  Long-time pointwise trajectory equality is not used
after chaotic decorrelation.

Historical symbols are attached only in a separately frozen source-geometric
test.  The critical retained from the unimodal side defines $C$ and the added
critical defines $D$.  The interval beyond the added critical is assigned the
third-branch symbol, while the deposited interval must independently be the
unique closest interval to the small equilibrium in physical state space.
Both assignments must agree across $x$ and $z$, disjoint return segments, and
DOP853/Radau trajectories before any target-cycle word is evaluated.

Inside regular windows, the attracting orbit cannot supply the chaotic return
map.  We therefore begin with a survival-ensemble sprinkler construction:
sample a declared section region, reject trajectories only after repeated
capture by the stable cycle, and retain middle-time states of long-lived
survivors as a finite approximation of the saddle
\citep{kantz1985repellers}.  Survivor and captured classifications are audited
pointwise against adaptive DOP853 only over short horizons.  Over long chaotic
horizons, robustness is assessed empirically through replicated survivor curves,
return-map geometry, and critical-point stability across time step,
conditioning window, grid phase, and grid resolution.  Candidate shallow
critical points must persist across a frozen binning/smoothing matrix and
bootstrap resampling in both section coordinates.  Regular-grid aliasing is
tested and then replaced by independently scrambled, nested Sobol ensembles;
section roots inside fixed RK4 steps are located with cubic Hermite
interpolation from endpoint vector fields.  Four bounded Newton updates refine
only the scalar within-step section root on that interpolant; RK4 remains the
state integrator.  Adaptive DOP853 provides the independent short-horizon
reference.  PIM-triple trajectories
provide a structurally different follow-up method rather than a tuning fallback
\citep{nusse1989procedure}.  For censor-aware PIM refinement, capture before
the observation ceiling gives an uncensored numerical capture lifetime, while
survival gives only a right-censored lower bound. A censored block is admissible only when it is
strictly interior and its lower bound exceeds the recorded lifetimes of captured
neighbors on both sides; boundary blocks and integration failures remain
unresolved.  Repeating the complete construction at frozen lifetime horizons
tests whether the scalar branch classification and sampled-domain coverage
depend on that ceiling.

PIM trajectories are used only to propose close-return seeds for unstable
periodic orbits.  A seed is retained only after numerically resolved section returns,
phase-conditioned flow shooting, independent monodromy integration, a neutral
Floquet multiplier, transverse instability, and the declared section identity
all pass.  Every proper integer divisor of the reported lag is tested for
closure, and primitive survivors are deduplicated by continuous phase-
invariant whole-orbit RMS.  Natural continuation then retains independent flow,
Floquet, primitivity, and section checks at every parameter point.  To avoid an
arbitrary orbit phase changing a half-open crossing count, the section identity
is counted over the shifted full-period interval $(0.1T,1.1T]$.  Optimizer
convergence is recorded separately from the prospectively declared scientific
closure bound.

To obtain a signed companion to the integer branch count, we affinely normalize
each scalar PIM return relation and evaluate the fitted-spline derivative at
the first populated binned-source median.  This avoids extrapolation outside
the occupied support.  A sign resolves only when every frozen binning and
smoothing variant agrees and the smallest absolute normalized derivative
exceeds a preregistered floor.  The observable remains section- and coordinate-
bounded, and it shares state data with the branch oracle; it is therefore a
candidate continuation coordinate rather than an independent topological
invariant.

Branch-conditioned capture is tested by freezing critical-point sensitivity
bands from prior return-map data and excluding those bands from later branch
assignment.  Each new trajectory contributes only its last section crossing
before a fixed landmark and is followed from that landmark to attractor capture
or a common administrative censor.  The primary contrast is extra-minus-core
restricted mean survival time. Three independent Sobol scrambles and a
same-ensemble half-step calculation provide replication and numerical controls.
Repeated crossings are not counted as independent observations, but points
within one scrambled Sobol net are still dependent. The retained within-net
trajectory-bootstrap ranges and log-rank statistics are therefore descriptive
sensitivity diagnostics: their nominal levels are not asserted to have
calibrated confidence coverage or type-I error for this sampling design.
Formal ensemble uncertainty would require an analysis using independent
scrambles as the replication units; it has not been established here.

Likewise, repeated branch-classifier fits over bins, smoothing scales, and
bootstrap resamples reuse trajectory clouds. Their agreement is a robustness
matrix, not a count of independent replications or independent evidence.

The identified historical-section grazing exposes a separate unfinished audit.
A systematic archive-wide check of normalized crossing transversality,
$|n\!\cdot f|/(\|n\|\,\|f\|)$, and comparison with extremum-aware event detection
has not been completed. The dedicated grazing checks qualify their selected
orbit family; they do not retroactively certify every older section-based
