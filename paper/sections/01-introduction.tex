\section{Why do periodic windows form spirals?}
\label{sec:question}

A chaotic system can become regular when a parameter changes by a small
amount. In two-parameter plots, those regular regions often resemble shrimp:
a central body with narrow extensions, surrounded by other periodic windows.
In the R{\"o}ssler system, families of these windows wind into nested spirals.
A \emph{periodicity hub} is the apparent organizing center of such a spiral.
A useful explanation must account for the connections between windows, not
only reproduce their appearance.

We study the R{\"o}ssler equations \citep{rossler1976equation},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rossler}
  \dot{x}=-y-z,\qquad
  \dot{y}=x+ay,\qquad
  \dot{z}=b+z(x-c),
\end{equation}
where $(x,y,z)$ is the state and $(a,b,c)$ are parameters. Most of the
historical question concerns the $(a,c)$ plane at $b=0.2$.
Figure~\ref{fig:multib-superstructure} extends the view across eleven values
of $b$. Curved bands persist and change shape, suggesting a shared
organization. The images alone cannot tell us which invariant objects
produce it.

Earlier work connected spiral organization to saddle-focus homoclinic
geometry and changes in return-map topology \citep{barrio2011global}.
The two 2012 studies are treated here as independent, near-simultaneous
co-discoveries of the return-map transition's connection to periodicity
hubs. Jones proposed finite symbolic ordering and a reinjection explanation
for links between neighboring windows \citep{jones2012topological}.
Barrio, Blesa, and Serrano developed a topology-bifurcation and
double-superstability description \citep{barrio2012topological}.
The earlier 2011 attribution remains explicit.

Subsequent work developed spiral and homoclinic organization in R{\"o}ssler
and other flows \citep{barrio2013spirals,malykh2020homoclinic}.
Computer-assisted studies also supply rigorous periodic-orbit results and
methods for validating homoclinic connections in specified settings
\citep{gierzkiewicz2021periodic,capinski2017homoclinics,nitta2022verification}.
These are prior results and methodological benchmarks, not certifications
of the numerical candidates studied here. Parameter conventions differ
between papers and must be translated before comparing coordinates.
Our contribution is a reproducible reassessment of declared claims and
the tests that distinguish supported mechanisms from unresolved ones.

We ask three questions. Which local objects in this explanation can be
reproduced? Which observed changes belong to the dynamics, and which arise
from the way trajectories are measured? What remains necessary to turn local
agreement into a predictive explanation of the spirals?

The answer is partly positive and partly corrective. Periodic families,
several doublings, and local return-map branch distinctions reproduce.
Some earlier numerical interpretations do not survive stronger checks.
The main article follows those results and their implications; the technical
supplement preserves the full experiment sequence, including failed tests.
