\section{Why do periodic windows form spirals?}
\label{sec:question}

A chaotic system can become regular when a parameter changes by a small
amount. In two-parameter plots, those regular regions often resemble shrimp:
a central body with narrow extensions, surrounded by other periodic windows.
In the R{\"o}ssler system, families of these windows wind into nested spirals.
A \emph{periodicity hub} is the apparent organizing center of such a spiral.
A useful explanation must account for the connections between windows, not
only reproduce their appearance.

Jones's symbolic construction addresses exactly that requirement. It proposes
that moving around a spiral changes how an orbit returns, leaving one extra
inner visit in its repeating itinerary. If this mechanism is correct, it
should explain both the increase from period $p$ to $p+1$ and where the new
visit occurs in the symbolic word. That is a more specific prediction than
the existence of periodic windows or a period-doubling cascade. Testing it
is the central purpose of this paper. Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} explains
the construction, and Figure~\ref{fig:symbolic-chain} displays its finite
network of reported connections \citep{jones2012topological}.

We study the R{\"o}ssler equations \citep{rossler1976equation},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rossler}
  \dot{x}=-y-z,\qquad
  \dot{y}=x+ay,\qquad
  \dot{z}=b+z(x-c),
\end{equation}
where $(x,y,z)$ is the state and $(a,b,c)$ are parameters. Most of the
historical question concerns the $(a,c)$ plane at $b=0.2$.
Figure~\ref{fig:multib-superstructure} extends the view across eleven values
of $b$. Curved bands persist and change shape, suggesting a shared
organization. The images alone cannot tell us which invariant objects
produce it.
Jones reported morphology over $b=0.2$--$2.0$
\citep{jones2012topological}. Our denser local set of slices does not test
that full range or establish a broader robustness result.

Periodicity hubs were already described in nonlinear circuit models
\citep{bonatto2008periodicity}. R{\"o}ssler bifurcation studies mapped local
and global structures \citep{barrio2009qualitative}; Vitolo, Glendinning,
and Gallas specifically related hub spirals to folded sheaves of Shilnikov
homoclinic bifurcations \citep{vitolo2011global}. Barrio and colleagues
also connected spiral organization to saddle-focus homoclinic geometry
\citep{barrio2011global}. Those homoclinic objects are not the period-6
flip curve computed here (Table~\ref{tab:distinct-loci}).
The two 2012 studies are treated here as independent, near-simultaneous
co-discoveries of the return-map transition's connection to periodicity
hubs. Jones proposed finite symbolic ordering and a reinjection explanation
for links between neighboring windows \citep{jones2012topological}.
Barrio, Blesa, and Serrano developed a topology-bifurcation and
double-superstability description \citep{barrio2012topological}.
The earlier 2011 attribution remains explicit.

Subsequent work developed spiral and homoclinic organization in R{\"o}ssler
and other flows \citep{barrio2013spirals,malykh2020homoclinic}.
Computer-assisted studies also supply rigorous periodic-orbit results and
methods for validating homoclinic connections in specified settings
\citep{gierzkiewicz2021periodic,capinski2017homoclinics,nitta2022verification}.
These are prior results and methodological benchmarks, not certifications
of the numerical candidates studied here. Parameter conventions differ
between papers and must be translated before comparing coordinates.
R{\"o}ssler-specific symbolic and template analysis also predates this study
\citep{letellier1995unstable}. More recent work rigorously establishes many
periodic windows using symbolic orbit search, continuation, and interval
methods \citep{galias2025symbolic}, while analytical work on an idealized
heteroclinic model addresses a different set of assumptions
\citep{igra2025knots}. Appendix~\ref{supp:prior-work} compares these results
and their mathematical objects. Our contribution is a computational
reassessment centered on the explanatory
step from return geometry to symbolic ordering, with explicit separation
between source claims, successful local reconstructions, and missing tests.

We ask three linked questions. Can an independently reconstructed partition
recover the reported finite words? Can measured reinjection predict the
extra return between neighboring windows? Can those connections account for
the larger spiral organization without confusing changes of measurement
with changes of the dynamics?

The answer is not yet a reproduced symbolic chain. The local alphabet has
support, but the critical itineraries and their connections remain open.
Orbit continuation, saddle reconstruction, and homoclinic calculations test
supporting ingredients; agreement on separate parameter slices does not
join those ingredients into one demonstrated mechanism. The main article
therefore presents the symbolic hypothesis before the methods and numerical
results. The technical supplement preserves the full experiment sequence,
including failed tests and corrections to the modern reconstruction.
The present paper is a claim-centered audit directed toward a symbolic
explanation, not a completed proof of that explanation. Public reproduction
is currently demonstrated only for the core subset in
Section~\ref{sec:availability}.
