\section{Introduction}

The R{\"o}ssler equations provide a compact continuous-time system in which
chaotic attractors, periodic windows, homoclinic organization, and symbolic
dynamics can be studied together \citep{rossler1976equation}.  Two-parameter
scans reveal periodic windows arranged in shrimp-like families and nested
spirals.  The organizing center of such a spiral is commonly called a
\emph{periodicity hub}.

Figure~\ref{fig:multib-superstructure} makes the principal geometric object
visible before we introduce the orbit-level tests.  Across eleven fixed-$b$
slices, the broad families persist while bending, merging in projection, and
changing their internal period decoration.  This is the superstructure that
the proposed Hopf, cascade, return-map, and reinjection mechanisms must
explain; no single raster pixel is treated as a bifurcation certificate.

The historical claim boundary matters.  Barrio, Blesa, Serrano, and Shilnikov
identified a global organization of spiral structures and a transition between
return-map topologies before the two 2012 papers \citep{barrio2011global}.
Jones then connected a two/three-branch transition, finite symbolic ordering,
and a proposed reinjection mechanism to the nested R{\"o}ssler spirals
\citep{jones2012topological}.  Independently and at nearly the same time,
Barrio, Blesa, and Serrano connected the topology bifurcation of attractors
(TBA) to periodicity hubs and doubly superstable shrimp centers
\citep{barrio2012topological}.  We therefore treat the 2012 topology--hub
connection as an independent, near-simultaneous co-discovery while keeping the
earlier 2011 attribution explicit.

The original Jones language also made the relationship to a logistic map too
strong.  Similar finite kneading order in experimental flow data was known at
least through period eight \citep{lefranc1994combining}, whereas a genuinely
two-dimensional return map permits infinitely many high-period bifurcation
orders that differ from a one-dimensional unimodal map
\citep{holmes1984bifurcation}.  Accordingly, this paper does not assume global
conjugacy or universal ordering.  It asks which explicitly defined finite
correspondences survive computation, where the first disagreement occurs, and
whether a measurable reinjection observable predicts structure beyond the
already known TBA and homoclinic organization.

Our immediate contribution is narrower and testable.  We reconstruct the
R{\"o}ssler computation with provenance-bearing experiments, continue exact
periodic orbits instead of reading bifurcations from pixels, resolve a deep
local period-doubling cascade, and distinguish a flow bifurcation from a
change induced by the chosen return section.  The central topology and
reinjection claims remain global prospective tests, while a local sampled
saddle-topology bracket is now qualified below.
