\section{Mathematical objects and claim scope}

We study the flow $\Phi^t_{a,b,c}$ generated by
\begin{align}
  \dot{x} &= -y-z, \\
  \dot{y} &= x+a y, \\
  \dot{z} &= b+z(x-c),
\end{align}
with state $(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^3$ and parameters $(a,b,c)$.  Parameter-plane
experiments state which parameter is fixed before defining the scanned domain;
the period-5 cascade below fixes $(a,c)=(0.245,5.1)$ and varies $b$.
The Jones-path reconstruction instead fixes $(a,b)=(0.1798,0.2)$ and varies
$c$, while the returning period-6 cascade fixes
$(b,c)=(0.2,7.625815600403827)$ and varies $a$.

For a declared transverse section $\Sigma$, the first-return map is the
two-dimensional map
\begin{equation}
  P_{a,b,c}:U\subset\Sigma\longrightarrow\Sigma,
  \qquad P(q)=\Phi^{\tau(q)}_{a,b,c}(q),
\end{equation}
where $\tau(q)>0$ is the first admissible return time.  A plotted scalar return
relation $u_{n+1}$ versus $u_n$ is a projection of $P$ unless an invariant
one-dimensional graph or quotient has been independently established.  We
therefore distinguish the flow, section, return map, sampled invariant set,
coordinate projection, and symbolic partition throughout.

We denote the minimal positive flow return time by $T$.  Integer labels such
as ``period 6'' retain the historical cycle label, checked by the stated
winding or section-return convention; they do not mean $T=6$.  A
section-return period is the number of iterates of the declared first-return
map needed to close the orbit.  These integers need not agree across sections:
the historical period-6 family below has eight returns on the Barrio section.
A section-crossing count can change
when an orbit grazes a section boundary even though the flow orbit remains
regular and its Floquet multipliers do not cross the unit circle.  The terms
\emph{conjugacy}, \emph{template}, and \emph{universality} are reserved for
claims whose hypotheses and tested objects are stated explicitly.  The current
evidence supports none of those words as an unqualified global description.

One critical point gives a unimodal scalar map with two monotone branches;
two critical points give a bimodal scalar map with three branches.  Critical
points of a scalar projection must not be confused with singularities of the
full return map.  For a regular periodic orbit of this smooth flow,
Liouville's formula \citep{teschl2012ordinary} gives
\[
  \det D\Phi^T(q)
  =\exp\!\left(\int_0^T [a+x(t)-c]\,dt\right)>0.
\]
The autonomous neutral multiplier is one, so the product of the two transverse
Floquet multipliers is also strictly positive.  Neither transverse multiplier
can be exactly zero at finite $T$.  Thus ``superstability'' of a scalar
return-map model is a property of that model or quotient, not an exact
zero-multiplier property of the full flow.
