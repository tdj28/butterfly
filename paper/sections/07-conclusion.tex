\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The shrimp patterns are no longer only pictures: parts of their periodic-orbit
structure and the associated return maps can now be followed and checked
directly. The calculations also explain why long chaotic transients and
section choices can mislead a finite-time scan.

For Jones's proposal, the result is encouraging but incomplete. Several local
ingredients reproduce, a nearby homoclinic candidate is supported, and a
saddle-lobe association gives a concrete direction for testing reinjection.
The global topology-transition curve, finite symbolic ordering, and
window-to-window explanation remain open. The modern reconstruction itself
has required corrections, which are preserved in the technical record.

The next advance should connect geometry to prediction: independently
validate the homoclinic calculation, measure the manifold event behind the
branch change, and reproduce the finite symbolic links in a readable
unfolded-spiral figure. Success on those questions would strengthen the
scientific explanation more than simply enlarging the atlas.
