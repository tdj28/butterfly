\section{Conclusion}

In plain terms, the modern code can now follow individual periodic motions
through eight successive doublings and can tell when a seeming change belongs
to the dynamics rather than to the way the dynamics were sampled.  That is
substantial new support for a computable local structure near the periodicity
windows.  It also verifies a locally robust three-branch return relation on
the historically relevant oriented half-plane while exposing the limits of
scalar representations on the opposite half-plane.

It also now reconstructs the nonattracting chaotic saddles coexisting with the
published period-4 controls.  Across scrambled and nested ensembles, step and
horizon changes, two coordinates, and a frozen oracle matrix, the left control
has two branches and the right control has three.  This closes a central local
gap left by attractor-only scans.  A prospectively gated Float64 GPU transfer
reproduces the same distinction and survivor curves, while retaining the
distinction between a qualified pair of controls and a global topology-
transition curve.

A structurally independent PIM construction with adaptive DOP853 now recovers the
same two/three distinction at a fixed 128-return lifetime ceiling, with all
oracle variants agreeing and critical locations consistent with the sprinkler.
Its 64-return profiles under-cover the invariant set, so this is independent
fixed-horizon corroboration rather than lifetime-horizon convergence.

It is not yet the promised explanation of the whole butterfly-like parameter
plane.  The key remaining question is whether the extra branch of the return
map has a reproducible reinjection geometry that predicts how neighboring
periodic windows connect.  The next phase tests that question directly, with a
fixed return-map definition, uncertainty on every branch decision, held-out
predictions, and a new unfolded-spiral figure that a reader can reconstruct
from the experiment receipts.
