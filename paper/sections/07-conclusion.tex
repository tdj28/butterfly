\section{Conclusion}

The reported hub coordinate has the claimed local saddle-focus eigenstructure.
This closes the equilibrium classification but not the much stronger claim
that the coordinate lies on a unique homoclinic connection.
The regular small-equilibrium Hopf locus is now exact and independently
qualified; at the hub abscissa it begins at $c=0.5192306257$.  This supplies
the local start of the proposed fixed-$a$ Hopf-to-homoclinic construction,
and the same one-winding period-1 family is independently followed to the exact
hub with the expected square-root amplitude law and a first stability loss.
The first flip is now coupled and independently resolved at
$c=3.1807265333384103$.  A local nullspace switch and independent Radau
qualification establish its primitive stable period-2 child at $c=3.1845$,
including parent-to-child stability exchange and perturbed-attractor recovery.
Later rungs and finite symbolic ordering remain open.
Its finite hub period and large equilibrium separation show that this orbit is
not itself the proposed homoclinic connection.  The global equilibrium-manifold
endpoint remains open.

In plain terms, the modern code can now follow individual periodic motions
through eight successive doublings and can tell when a seeming change belongs
to the dynamics rather than to the way the dynamics were sampled.  That is
substantial new support for a computable local structure near the periodicity
windows.  It also verifies a locally robust three-branch return relation on
the historically relevant oriented half-plane while exposing the limits of
scalar representations on the opposite half-plane.

It also now reconstructs the nonattracting chaotic saddles coexisting with the
published period-4 controls.  Across scrambled and nested ensembles, step and
horizon changes, two coordinates, and a frozen oracle matrix, the left control
has two branches and the right control has three.  This closes a central local
gap left by attractor-only scans.  A prospectively gated Float64 GPU transfer
reproduces the same distinction and survivor curves, while retaining the
distinction between a qualified pair of controls and a global topology-
transition curve.

Independent adaptive-DOP853 PIM recovers the same distinction and preserves
all six straddles, branch counts, and critical locations from 128 to 256
returns; the 64-return profile fails coverage.  A blind point at $a=0.140$
passes as two-branch.  After an eightfold support study isolates $a=0.145$
failures to 80-bin $z$ coverage, a control-qualified prospective censor rule
qualifies that saddle as two and narrows the bracket to $[0.145,0.149]$.  At
$a=0.150$, positive-Lyapunov chaos and
new-data resolution convergence now qualify three branches and show why its
old coarse label returned two.  Adaptive saddle continuation inside and beyond
the new bracket is the next continuity test; its first blind midpoint at
$a=0.147$ passes as two-branch and halves the interval to $[0.147,0.149]$.
The next midpoint at $a=0.148$ first exposes different branch geometry under
300- and 360-unit survivor conditioning.  A nested 360--480 audit finds two
branches in all 228 resolved cells but retains 12 rare-survivor bootstrap
failures.  Independent blind PIM then resolves the ambiguity: both 128- and
256-return saddle reconstructions unanimously select two, narrowing the finite
sampled bracket to $[0.148,0.149]$.  The identical blind method then selects
three at $a=0.1485$ at both horizons, localizing the finite classifier boundary
to $[0.148,0.1485]$; a second blind three-branch midpoint at $a=0.14825$
halves it to $[0.148,0.14825]$.  A prospective branch-conditioned test then rejects
faster capture at $a=0.148$: the transient extra branch persists longer on
average but has a bounded tail, explaining why it vanishes under deeper
conditioning without weakening the PIM bracket.
The held-out $a=0.148125$ successor then passes as two-branch, while every
frozen lower-support slope is negative and predicts the same class.  The later
blind $a=0.1481875$ midpoint also passes as two at both horizons, narrowing the
finite bracket to $[0.1481875,0.14825]$.  The signed observable supplies a
candidate continuation coordinate, not yet a global curve.
The first transverse extension also produces a useful negative result.  Brute
GPU sprinklers retain adequate support and reproduce remote controls but do not
resolve the narrow PIM boundary.  Adaptive PIM then falsifies the proposed
three-branch endpoint at $(c,a)=(19.8,0.148)$ while fully resolving
$(19.9,0.150)$ as three.  The evidence therefore favors a bent or displaced
boundary over a nearly vertical continuation, while establishing neither its
continuous locus nor its manifold mechanism.  At 256 returns, the complete
$c=19.9$ finite bracket $[0.145,0.150]$ and the new
$(c,a)=(19.8,0.150)$ three-branch endpoint pass prospectively.  The
$(19.8,0.148)$ endpoint remains bootstrap-unresolved, so the transverse result
strengthens the local picture without promoting a second bracket or a curve.

An orbit-level census sharpens that remaining limitation.  Phase-conditioned
shooting, proper-divisor reduction, continuous whole-orbit identity, and
Floquet audits recover eleven primitive UPO families from the two endpoint
saddles.  Every family continues across the local bracket with its fundamental
section lag intact.  The branch opening is therefore not a simple birth, death,
or section-crossing loss among the recovered periodic skeleton; a global
manifold/pruning or reinjection event remains to be identified.  Phase-resolved
manifold seeds validate, but a prospective four-branch refinement rejects
finite-horizon stable-cycle capture timing as a robust proxy for that event.
The required next object is therefore a direct, convergent manifold-intersection
or symbolic-pruning residual.  A retrospective full-section diagnostic takes
one step in that direction: a pre-existing unstable lobe is excluded from the
two-branch PIM saddle but represented in every three-branch reconstruction,
with nested-distance agreement to the UPO atlas.  A prospectively frozen
untouched midpoint then passes the predicted two-branch/lobe-excluded
association on all six PIM clouds.  This supports pruning or reinjection while
leaving the exact manifold contact open.

It is not yet the promised explanation of the whole butterfly-like parameter
plane.  The key question is whether invariant extra-branch reinjection
predicts neighboring-window connections.  The next phase fixes the return map
and uncertainty, then tests held-out predictions and an unfolded-spiral figure.
