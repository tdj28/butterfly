\section{What the evidence shows}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The broad pattern and the individual windows are different tests}

The multi-$b$ atlas shows a coherent family of patterns
(Figure~\ref{fig:multib-superstructure}). It also shows why the global problem
is difficult: narrow periodic bands are embedded in a much larger region
whose finite-time behavior is often unresolved. A convincing explanation
must connect individual invariant orbits to that larger picture.

At $b=0.2$, a refined scan resolves two historical period-6 landmarks
(Figure~\ref{fig:global-zoom}). They lie in different connected components
of the sampled stable raster. The component around the second landmark
contains 2,598 pixels and spans approximately $c=7.124$ to $8.192$.
This establishes separation at the chosen resolution. It does not exclude a
narrower bridge or a connection through unstable periodic orbits.
% Evidence: EXP-191 and subsequent period-6 component preparation.

  \caption{From the broad parameter region to one periodic window. Left:
  the reported hub and two period-6 landmarks are marked in the $b=0.2$
  atlas. Right: a scan with 50 times finer spacing in each parameter
  resolves the band around the second landmark; the black outline selects
  its sampled period-6 component. The separation from the first landmark

\subsection{Two and three branches can be reconstructed}

Figure~\ref{fig:return-map-controls} makes the branch distinction concrete.
For two published attracting controls at $(b,c)=(0.2,20)$, the return
relation has either one or two turning points. This reproduces the
unimodal/bimodal contrast on the declared section
\citep{barrio2012topological}.

\begin{figure}[tbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig07-return-map-controls.png}
  \caption{What a change from two branches to three looks like. Each
  point pairs a crossing coordinate with its next value. The left control
  has one turning point; the right has two. Dashed lines locate the
  turning points and the red line is the fitted return relation.
  These well-separated attracting controls establish the measurement
  convention. The much narrower saddle-transition interval discussed in
  the text requires separate nonattracting-set calculations.}
  \label{fig:return-map-controls}
\end{figure}

Inside a periodic window, a typical trajectory eventually repeats and cannot
by itself reveal the surrounding chaotic saddle. In our tested cases,
some trajectories first appeared chaotic but later entered the same stable
periodic orbit. That removes those trajectories as evidence for coexisting
chaotic attractors; it does not rule out every possible basin of attraction.
Long transients must therefore be distinguished from persistent multistability.

Independent saddle reconstructions give a stronger result. At
$(b,c)=(0.2,20)$, the last tested two-branch point is $a=0.1481875$ and the
adjacent tested three-branch point is $a=0.14825$. The classifications survive
two censor horizons and the prescribed reconstruction checks. They delimit
a sampled transition interval, not a rigorously enclosed bifurcation or proof
that only one transition lies inside it.
% Evidence: EXP-112--132, EXP-148, EXP-149.

What changes within that interval? Eleven recovered unstable periodic-orbit
families persist across it. None of those families is created or destroyed
at the sampled branch change. Instead, a pre-existing unstable lobe is
represented in all tested three-branch saddle clouds and excluded from the
tested two-branch clouds. An untouched midpoint reproduces that association.
The result favors a change in which parts of the existing structure can be
visited---a pruning or reinjection mechanism---over a birth or death of one
of the recovered orbits. A direct manifold-contact calculation remains open.
% Evidence: EXP-133--145 and EXP-148/149.

\subsection{A connected periodic family supplies a local skeleton}

A Hopf bifurcation creates a small periodic orbit near an equilibrium.
At fixed $(a,b)=(0.1798,0.2)$, the analytic small-equilibrium Hopf point is
$c_H\simeq0.5192306257$. The emerging period-1 family can be followed to the
reported hub coordinate (Figure~\ref{fig:hopf-family}). Along this explicit
reconstruction of the Jones path, four corrected flips occur at
\[
  c\simeq3.1807265333,\quad4.3100451385,\quad
  4.6364472010,\quad4.7090113824.
\]
Independent child checks establish stable primitive orbits through period 16.
These are finite period-doubling results; they do not establish the ordering
of every symbolic orbit claimed in the original paper.
% Evidence: EXP-153--173.

\begin{figure}[tbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig04-hopf-family.png}
  \caption{Following one family instead of guessing from pixel colors.
  The periodic orbit born at the Hopf point grows and changes its return
  time as $c$ increases on the fixed-$a$ path. Independent corrections
  check that the same family is being followed. Its period doublings are
  located from stability equations and separately checked daughters.
  Reaching the reported hub coordinate does not make this periodic orbit
  a homoclinic orbit.}
  \label{fig:hopf-family}
\end{figure}

A second calculation follows the period-doubling boundary of the period-6
window itself. The resulting locus bends back in the $(a,c)$ plane, forming
two widely separated arms (Figure~\ref{fig:exp215-217-folded}). A sampled
period-12 child sheet connects locally to the first arm; further checks
recover stable children on part of the returning arm. At $c=8.25$, the
two parent-event arms are separated by about $0.05778$ in $a$.
The connected event locus is a substantial part of the local structure,
but it has not been shown that both complete arms bound the same shrimp.

A section-dependent change occurs along this continuation. Near
$c=6.9383180212$, the orbit grazes the historical section boundary and its
recorded crossing count changes. The underlying flip event and the
Barrio-section count persist. The apparent count change therefore belongs
to the measurement. It is distinct from the later turn of the parameter
curve near $c=6.83093$.
% Evidence: EXP-203--229; EXP-229 retracts the distinct second-boundary inference.

\subsection{The cascades extend deeply, but high period exposes false identities}

There are three different parameter paths in the record. The Hopf-born
Jones-path family above fixes $(a,b)$ and varies $c$. A period-5 cascade
fixes $(a,c)=(0.245,5.1)$ and varies $b$, reaching eight locally checked
supercritical doublings and a stable period-1280 child. The returning
period-6 cascade fixes $(b,c)=(0.2,7.625815600403827)$ and varies $a$.
Their period counts and parameter spacings must not be combined.
% Evidence: EXP-056--106; EXP-153--173; EXP-230--327.

The first six returning-arm doublings have independently checked stable
children through period 768 (Figure~\ref{fig:exp237-275-returning-cascade}).
Higher-precision calculations recover a seventh supercritical birth and a
connected eighth event. Evidence for the eighth birth's subcritical direction
is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:claims}; it requires further local testing.
The finite event-spacing ratios are not a demonstration of universality.

\begin{figure}[tbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig22-exp237-275-returning-cascade.png}
  \caption{The first six checked doublings of the returning family.
  Upper panels show event locations and finite spacing ratios.
  Lower panels compare the loss of parent stability with the stability
  of the doubled children, using independent integrators.
  This figure stops at the stable period-768 child. The technical
  supplement records the subsequent high-precision events, the false
  daughter that was retracted, and the remaining criticality question.}
  \label{fig:exp237-275-returning-cascade}
\end{figure}

The deepest calculations also provide a caution. One apparently stable
period-1536 daughter survived several ordinary checks, but stronger
same-map correction drove its half-orbit separation to zero. It was the
period-768 parent followed twice. A distinct immediate daughter was then
recovered and connected to the next event. This corrects the modern
reconstruction; it does not refute period doubling itself.
% Evidence: EXP-318--327; source EXP-299 primitive identity retracted.

\subsection{A homoclinic candidate is supported near the reported hub}

At the printed coordinate $(a,b,c)=(0.1798,0.2,10.3084)$, the small
equilibrium has eigenvalues approximately
$0.08897\pm0.99596i$ and $-10.30304$. It therefore has the proposed
saddle-focus structure: outward spiral motion in two directions and strong
contraction in one. That local calculation is necessary for the proposed
mechanism, but does not show a global return to the equilibrium.

Direct scans at the printed point do not find a qualifying connection.
A separate boundary-value calculation instead matches outgoing trajectories
to the nonlinear stable manifold near
\[
  (a,b,c)\simeq(0.182643608174,0.2,10.3084).
\]
DOP853 and Radau, different shooting meshes, and three matching radii support
this candidate (Figure~\ref{fig:homoclinic-root}). Its $a$ coordinate differs
from the printed value by about $0.00284$, exceeding four-decimal rounding.
Finding it neither excludes another connection at the printed point nor
establishes that it organizes the observed hub.
% Evidence: EXP-329--346.

\begin{figure}[tbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig29-exp342-346-homoclinic-root.png}
  \caption{A nearby candidate leaves the saddle focus and returns toward
  its stable manifold. Left and center: the matched trajectory in two
  projections, with color showing flight time. Right: shrinking the
  matching sphere preserves the fitted parameter while the arc defects
  remain below the declared numerical threshold. The dashed line is
  Jones's printed $a$. Agreement across these checks supports the
  candidate; it does not prove existence, uniqueness, or the candidate's

  \label{fig:homoclinic-root}
\end{figure}

Subsequent continuation produces 81 accepted numerical points and an apparent
local parameter turn. The closest sampled point lies about
$1.75\times10^{-5}$ above the exact fixed-$a=0.1798$ slice; the outgoing
samples then move away. The detailed plot in
Figure~\ref{fig:homoclinic-continuation} preserves the 80-point EXP-472
snapshot, with EXP-473 recorded in the accompanying text.
The small Jacobian singular values mean that residual size alone does not
bound the parameter error at the scale of this gap. A changed formulation,
truncation study, and parameter-sensitive error estimate are therefore
required before assigning a precise physical location to the turn.
The printed intersection, uniqueness, and global nonintersection remain open.
