We reassess and extend the proposed topological explanation of periodicity
hubs in the R{\"o}ssler flow using provenance-bearing parameter scans,
periodic-orbit continuation, independent integrators, Floquet analysis, and
return-map reconstructions.  Multi-slice $(a,c)$ atlases at
$b=0.10,\ldots,0.30$ expose a smoothly deforming superstructure of shrimp-like
periodic windows and provide the visual context for the orbit-level tests.
At the reported hub, the small equilibrium is a saddle focus.  Its exact Hopf
locus supplies a verified local endpoint, and the emerging period-1 family is
continued to the reported hub coordinate.  Four exact supercritical flips and
stable children through period 16 are established on this path; the orbit at
the hub nevertheless remains finite-period and far from the equilibrium, so
it is not itself the proposed homoclinic connection.  On a second path,
identity-safe continuation resolves eight period doublings of a period-5
family through a stable period-1280 child.

On the section used by Barrio, Blesa, and Serrano, the published one- and
two-critical-point return-map controls are reproduced.  Censor-aware
straddle reconstructions of the corresponding nonattracting chaotic saddles
localize a sampled two/three-branch transition at
$a\in[0.1481875,0.14825]$ for $(b,c)=(0.2,20)$.  Recovered unstable periodic
orbits persist across this bracket, while a pre-existing unstable lobe joins
the reconstructed saddle only on the three-branch side, favoring a global
pruning or reinjection mechanism over a local orbit birth or death.

High-resolution scans around two historical period-6 landmarks place them in
distinct stable raster components.  Fifty-eight corrected period-6 flow
orbits show that historical period 6 appears as an eight-phase cycle on the
published section.  None of the sampled representatives satisfies the frozen
double-critical-center test; a denser corrected-orbit mesh localizes a
connected candidate region but fails its preregistered coverage gate.  On its
126 two-step-qualified maps, one signed critical residual crosses zero but the
other remains strictly positive, so neither a direct center nor a simultaneous
bracket is present in the incomplete sampled field.  Thus the computations
substantially strengthen the finite cascade and return-map parts of the Jones
picture while keeping the homoclinic endpoint, the global double-critical
center claim, a continuous topology-transition curve, and a complete
explanation of the full parameter plane open.
