Periodic windows in the R{\"o}ssler flow form striking shrimp-like patterns
and nested spirals. The question is whether changes in the underlying return
map explain that organization. We revisit the Jones and
Barrio--Blesa--Serrano studies using corrected periodic orbits, independent
integrators, and reconstructions of chaotic sets that do not attract nearby
trajectories.

The computations support a local account of the pattern. Eleven parameter-plane
slices show coherent changes in the periodic-window structure. A Hopf-born
family undergoes four checked period doublings on a reconstruction of the
Jones path. A period-6 family has a connected, folded period-doubling boundary
and a deep returning cascade. On a separate slice, the reconstructed chaotic
saddle changes from a two-branch to a three-branch scalar return relation
within a narrow sampled interval. Eleven recovered unstable periodic-orbit
families persist across that interval, while one unstable lobe appears in
the saddle samples only on the three-branch side.

A boundary-value calculation also supports a nearby homoclinic candidate,
although it does not establish the printed Jones coordinate, uniqueness, or
the candidate's role in organizing the hub. The audit retracts a false
high-period daughter and distinguishes section-dependent measurements from
changes in the flow. These results sharpen the proposed mechanism without
yet providing a complete topology-transition curve, finite symbolic
enumeration, or explanation of the whole parameter plane.
