\section{Topology and symbolic tests still required}

The original Jones mechanism proposes that, while a stable orbit is followed
around a nested parameter-space spiral, the dynamically relevant return
relation alternates between two and three branches and the third branch changes
its reinjection geometry \citep{jones2012topological}.  The Barrio analysis
provides a more explicit TBA and double-superstability construction
\citep{barrio2012topological}.  The modern experiments have now qualified one
local three-branch control, but have not yet established that global curve or
the reinjection mechanism.

The first test now supplies a gated branch-count oracle on a frozen section and
invariant domain.  It locates prominent critical points, rejects non-graph-like
or poorly covered projections, and requires bootstrap agreement.  Its first
R{"o}ssler sensitivity matrix passes for the historical negative-oriented map
in both $x$ and $z$, while correctly refusing most positive-half scalar
classifications.  The remaining task is to continue the prospective
two/three-branch boundary.  A second frozen control on the section declared by
Barrio, Blesa, and Serrano now reproduces their published unimodal and bimodal
attracting endpoints in both scalar coordinates.  Boundary continuation must
retain the full two-dimensional map and use a qualified chaotic-saddle method
inside regular windows.  An attractor-only scan now supplies an ordered
$[0.155,0.160]$ uncertainty band, but deliberately leaves the five sampled
period-4 windows unresolved.  A first sprinkler ensemble exposes the shallow
additional extremum at the published $a=0.149$ saddle control, but fails its
frozen global-prominence and pointwise-capture-agreement gates.  The next
prospective gate uses local critical-point uncertainty and ensemble-level
time-step, horizon, and grid convergence.  That gate now passes after
scrambled-Sobol sampling and cubic-Hermite event interpolation, directly
qualifying the published two/three saddle controls.  The remaining boundary
task now has fixed-128-return independent PIM corroboration, but the failed
64-return coverage control required a longer-horizon stability test.  That
test now passes at 256 returns; the remaining task is prospective saddle-
defined continuation between and beyond the controls.  Its first blind step
qualifies a two-branch saddle at $a=0.140$ but leaves $a=0.145$ support-
unresolved.  An audit of the adjacent $a=0.150$ candidate finds persistent
positive-Lyapunov chaos and a resolution split: coarse bins see two branches,
whereas every resolved 30--80-bin cell sees three.  New-data resolution
convergence now passes, qualifying the adequate-resolution three-branch label.
The enlarged $a=0.145$ ensemble removes the support deficit and returns two
branches in every resolved cell.  Its only failures are the 80-bin
$z$-coordinate variants, whose equal-width domains include invariant-support
gaps.  The next frozen test must qualify a coverage-censor rule prospectively
on both published controls and independent $a=0.145$ data before the saddle
bracket is narrowed or any boundary is fit.  That test requires
at least 12 of 15 variants to resolve normally, and every remainder must fail
only coverage while retaining at least 0.65 coverage, graph-likeness, the
expected nominal critical geometry, and the unchanged drift bounds.  It now
passes on all three new-data cases, qualifying $a=0.145$ as two-branch and
narrowing the sampled bracket to $[0.145,0.149]$.  The remaining task is
adaptive saddle continuation within and beyond that interval.  Its first
midpoint at $a=0.147$ is now frozen without an expected label: candidate counts
two and three must be uniquely selected by all seven new ensembles and both
coordinates.  That midpoint now passes unanimously as two-branch and halves
the sampled bracket to $[0.147,0.149]$; the next blind midpoint is frozen at
$a=0.148$ on independently sampled data.  It fails because 300-unit survivor
subsets expose three branches whereas the 360-unit subset exposes two.  Nested
conditioning through 480 time units then yields two in all 228 resolved oracle
cells and no three-branch result, but fails strict consensus because 12 80-bin
cells are bootstrap-unstable after rare-survivor attrition.  Independent
censor-aware PIM then targets the midpoint saddle directly and passes at both
128- and 256-return ceilings, selecting two in all 60 oracle cells and narrowing
the finite sampled bracket to $[0.148,0.149]$.  The identical blind method then
qualifies $a=0.1485$ as three at both censor ceilings, localizing the finite
classifier transition to $[0.148,0.1485]$.  A single
orbit's change in
historical crossing count remains insufficient,
particularly in light of the section grazing found above.

The second test recovers the original parameter paths $L_1$ and $L_2$ and
generates orbit permutations, kneading data, critical itineraries, and Floquet
stability through the eighth resolved doubling.  It then searches prospectively for the first
high-period mismatch, as required by the two-dimensional limitation emphasized
by Holmes \citep{holmes1984bifurcation}.  Agreement over a finite range will be
reported as finite correspondence, not conjugacy; the prior experimental
finite-order result of Lefranc et al. remains part of the novelty boundary
\citep{lefranc1994combining}.

The third test defines reinjection as an observable of the return map rather
than as the position of an abstract branched manifold in phase space.  It will
compare coordinates, branch order, critical values, winding/linking data, and
candidate template invariants.  Its decisive test is prospective: does the
observable predict held-out $p\mathbin{\to}p+1$ connections beyond predictions
already supplied by TBA and homoclinic geometry?

Finally, the original unfolded-spiral figure will be replaced by a generated,
receipt-bound figure.  Parameter arclength and angle, branch count, critical
values, periodic-orbit points, symbolic words, and both successful and
ambiguous transitions must be visible together.  Until those tests pass, the
topological mechanism remains a structured hypothesis.
