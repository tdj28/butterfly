\section{How the numerical evidence is checked}
\label{sec:methods}

The workflow moves from broad observation to targeted tests. Parameter scans
locate candidate windows. Shooting then adjusts an initial state and return
time until a candidate orbit closes, with a phase condition removing the
arbitrary choice of where its circuit starts. Continuation follows that
corrected orbit as a parameter changes. Its Floquet multipliers test
stability; a separate identity check rejects an apparent daughter that is
merely its parent traversed twice.

DOP853 and Radau supply independent time integrators for selected checks.
Newton corrections solve the shooting equations; they do not replace time
integration. Long orbits are divided into matched segments to limit
conditioning problems. The deepest cascade tests additionally use
50-digit arithmetic and two Runge--Kutta formulas at multiple step sizes.
The word \emph{exact} in the historical experiment record means directly
corrected rather than estimated by interpolation. It does not mean a
rigorous enclosure of a continuous-flow solution.

Chaotic saddles need a different approach. A trajectory that eventually
settles onto a periodic attractor may still spend a long time near a
nonattracting chaotic set \citep{kantz1985repellers}. We first reconstruct
that set from the middle portions of long-lived trajectories. Independent
PIM-triple calculations refine initial states that remain near the saddle
\citep{nusse1989procedure}. A finite observation ceiling is treated as
censoring, not proof of infinite survival.

A branch label is accepted only after the sampled return relation passes
coverage, projection-width, critical-point, and resampling checks. Time-step,
observation-horizon, smoothing, and coordinate changes test whether a label
depends on a numerical choice. An ambiguous case remains unresolved.

Each experiment stores its protocol, source identifiers, input hashes,
tolerances, numerical outputs, and failed acceptance checks. The present
paper summarizes scientific conclusions; Appendices~\ref{supp:methods} and
\ref{supp:experiments} retain the technical sequence. Agreement between two
solvers is valuable evidence, but shared assumptions and ill-conditioning
can still produce shared errors. Small residuals therefore require
interpretation, not just counting.
