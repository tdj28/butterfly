\begin{abstract}
We reassess and extend the proposed dynamical explanation of periodicity hubs
in the R{\"o}ssler flow.  The computational program separates finite-time
parameter rasters from invariant periodic orbits, flow bifurcations from
return-section geometry, and established results from topology hypotheses.
At fixed $(a,c)=(0.245,5.1)$, identity-safe continuation and segmented
multiple shooting resolve eight supercritical period doublings from period 5
through a stable period-1280 child.  Three event locations predicted before
their dedicated searches agree with the subsequently corrected values.
Separately, a change
in the historical section-crossing count is caused by a section-boundary
grazing rather than another flow-orbit bifurcation.  These results strengthen
the finite cascade and expose an important measurement distinction.  At a
published chaotic control, a frozen sensitivity matrix also qualifies a local
three-branch return relation on the historical oriented half-plane in both
nondegenerate scalar coordinates.  On the distinct section declared by
Barrio, Blesa, and Serrano, a second frozen matrix directly reproduces their
published unimodal and bimodal chaotic-attractor controls in all 84 cells and
an attractor-only path scan brackets a branch-change band in
$a\in[0.155,0.160]$.  Scrambled survival ensembles additionally reconstruct
the published two- and three-branch nonattracting chaotic saddles inside two
period-4 windows; a Float64 GPU implementation reproduces their topology and
survivor distributions under prospectively frozen parity gates.  At a fixed
128-return censor horizon, independent adaptive-DOP853 PIM straddles also
recover both saddle topologies, and a prospectively frozen 256-return extension
preserves their branch counts and critical locations.  A shorter 64-return
profile fails invariant-domain coverage, identifying an inadequate observation
scale rather than contradicting the longer-horizon result.  A new-data,
control-qualified coverage-censor test then resolves a third regular-window
saddle at $a=0.145$ as two-branch and brackets the sampled saddle topology
change in $a\in[0.145,0.149]$; a subsequent blind midpoint at $a=0.147$
selects two branches in every run and coordinate.  After finite-time
conditioning sensitivity is exposed at $a=0.148$, blind censor-aware PIM at
128 and 256 returns independently qualifies its saddle as two-branch,
and the identical blind method qualifies $a=0.1485$ as three-branch, narrowing
the sampled bracket to $[0.148,0.1485]$.
The opposite Jones half-plane does not admit the same stable scalar reduction.
These results do not yet continue a continuous invariant-saddle transition
curve through the parameter plane or establish the proposed global
two/three-branch curve,
third-branch reinjection mechanism, logistic conjugacy, or an explanation of
the entire parameter plane.  We state the experiments required to decide
those remaining claims and preserve their provenance in a reproducible
research record.
\end{abstract}
