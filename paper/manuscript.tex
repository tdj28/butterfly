\begin{abstract}
We reassess and extend the proposed dynamical explanation of periodicity hubs
in the R{\"o}ssler flow.  The computational program separates finite-time
parameter rasters from invariant periodic orbits, flow bifurcations from
return-section geometry, and established results from topology hypotheses.
At fixed $(a,c)=(0.245,5.1)$, identity-safe continuation and segmented
multiple shooting resolve eight supercritical period doublings from period 5
through a stable period-1280 child.  Three event locations predicted before
their dedicated searches agree with the subsequently corrected values.
Separately, a change
in the historical section-crossing count is caused by a section-boundary
grazing rather than another flow-orbit bifurcation.  These results strengthen
the finite cascade and expose an important measurement distinction, but they
do not yet establish the proposed global two/three-branch transition,
third-branch reinjection mechanism, logistic conjugacy, or an explanation of
the entire parameter plane.  We state the experiments required to decide
those remaining claims and preserve their provenance in a reproducible
research record.
\end{abstract}
