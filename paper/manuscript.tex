\begin{abstract}
We reassess and extend the proposed dynamical explanation of periodicity hubs
in the R{\"o}ssler flow.  The computational program separates finite-time
parameter rasters from invariant periodic orbits, flow bifurcations from
return-section geometry, and established results from topology hypotheses.
The analytic equilibrium at the reported hub coordinate has eigenvalues
$0.0889668\pm0.9959555i$ and $-10.3030439$, qualifying the claimed local
saddle-focus while leaving homoclinic existence and uniqueness open.  At fixed
$(b,a)=(0.2,0.1798)$, the exact small-equilibrium Hopf point is
$c=0.5192306257$; 192 analytic points pass independent eigensystem and
transversality checks, providing a qualified local start but not a homoclinic
endpoint for the proposed slice.  The same one-winding period-1 family is then
continued through 118 points to the reported hub coordinate; its near-Hopf amplitude exponent
is $0.5017311$, and a coupled exact-derivative solve locates its first real
$-1$ multiplier event at $c=3.1807265333384103$.  A doubled-cover nullspace
switch and independent Radau qualification then establish a primitive stable
period-2 child at $c=3.1845$.  Identity-safe continuation locates its next
exact flip at $c=4.3100451384813105$, and a second switch and qualification
establish a stable primitive period-4 child at $c=4.318$.  Its next exact flip
at $c=4.636447200967924$ and the resulting stable period-8 child at $c=4.65$
complete a third qualified rung.  A fourth exact event at
$c=4.7090113823613065$ and stable period-16 child at $c=4.716$ extend the
sequence again.  At
the hub the orbit remains finite-period and far from the equilibrium, so it is
not itself the proposed homoclinic connection.  At fixed
$(a,c)=(0.245,5.1)$, identity-safe continuation and segmented
multiple shooting resolve eight supercritical period doublings from period 5
through a stable period-1280 child.  Three event locations predicted before
their dedicated searches agree with the subsequently corrected values.
Separately, a change
in the historical section-crossing count is caused by a section-boundary
grazing rather than another flow-orbit bifurcation.  These results strengthen
the finite cascade and expose an important measurement distinction.  At a
published chaotic control, a frozen sensitivity matrix also qualifies a local
three-branch return relation on the historical oriented half-plane in both
nondegenerate scalar coordinates.  On the distinct section declared by
Barrio, Blesa, and Serrano, a second frozen matrix directly reproduces their
published unimodal and bimodal chaotic-attractor controls in all 84 cells and
an attractor-only path scan brackets a branch-change band in
$a\in[0.155,0.160]$.  Scrambled survival ensembles additionally reconstruct
the published two- and three-branch nonattracting chaotic saddles inside two
period-4 windows; a Float64 GPU implementation reproduces their topology and
survivor distributions under prospectively frozen parity gates.  At a fixed
128-return censor horizon, independent adaptive-DOP853 PIM straddles also
recover both saddle topologies, and a prospectively frozen 256-return extension
preserves their branch counts and critical locations.  A shorter 64-return
profile fails invariant-domain coverage, identifying an inadequate observation
scale rather than contradicting the longer-horizon result.  A new-data,
control-qualified coverage-censor test then resolves a third regular-window
saddle at $a=0.145$ as two-branch and brackets the sampled saddle topology
change in $a\in[0.145,0.149]$; a subsequent blind midpoint at $a=0.147$
selects two branches in every run and coordinate.  After finite-time
conditioning sensitivity is exposed at $a=0.148$, blind censor-aware PIM at
128 and 256 returns independently qualifies its saddle as two-branch,
and the identical blind method qualifies $a=0.1485$ as three-branch, narrowing
the sampled bracket to $[0.148,0.1485]$.  A second identical-method blind
midpoint qualifies $a=0.14825$ as three and halves the bracket to
$[0.148,0.14825]$.  A prospectively frozen
branch-conditioned survival test then rejects faster capture for the transient
extra branch at $a=0.148$: its capture is delayed on average but has a bounded
tail, explaining the earlier conditioning split without changing the PIM
bracket.  Finally, a held-out signed lower-support derivative predicts the
blind two-branch PIM result at $a=0.148125$ in both coordinates and at both
censor horizons.  A later untouched midpoint at $a=0.1481875$ also resolves as
two-branch at both horizons, narrowing the finite bracket to
$[0.1481875,0.14825]$.
An unresolved-aware transverse GPU pilot then passes its published controls
but fails near that narrow PIM boundary, rejecting brute survivor clouds as a
continuation oracle.  A prospectively frozen adaptive-PIM successor at
$c=19.8$ and $19.9$ fully qualifies $(c,a)=(19.9,0.150)$ as three-branch with
positive edge slope but falsifies the proposed three-branch class at
$(19.8,0.148)$: 24 of 30 branch variants return two and every signed slope is
negative.  The local transition therefore cannot be extrapolated as a nearly
vertical parameter-space boundary.  Its prospectively frozen 256-return
successor then qualifies the finite transverse bracket
$a\in[0.145,0.150]$ at $c=19.9$ and independently qualifies
$(c,a)=(19.8,0.150)$ as three-branch/positive.  The proposed lower endpoint
at $(19.8,0.148)$ remains unclassified because its highest-bin decisions are
bootstrap-unstable, so the four-endpoint experiment and any continuous-curve
claim remain failed.  Finally, PIM-seeded shooting, primitive-
period reduction, continuous whole-orbit identity, and Floquet audits recover
eleven distinct primitive unstable-periodic-orbit families from the two local
endpoint saddles.  Every recovered family continues across the sampled
two/three-branch bracket with its fundamental section identity intact.  This
rules out simple birth, death, or crossing loss of the recovered periodic
skeleton and selects a global manifold/pruning or reinjection event for the
next mechanism test.  On the independent $c=19.9$ bracket, the unchanged
pipeline prospectively recovers seven distinct primitive families and
validates every local unstable-manifold seed.  Validated phase-resolved
unstable seeds then reject
finite-horizon capture timing as a robust event proxy, leaving a direct
manifold-intersection or symbolic-pruning residual as the next gate.  A
retrospective full-section comparison then finds that a pre-existing unstable
lobe is excluded from the two-branch PIM saddle but included in every
three-branch reconstruction.  A prospectively frozen untouched midpoint then
passes the predicted two-branch/lobe-excluded association on all six PIM
clouds, supporting pruning or reinjection.
The opposite Jones half-plane does not admit the same stable scalar reduction.
On the qualified negative half-plane, split-cloud controls establish neutral
two- and three-branch partitions.  A fresh two-solver path tracks the unimodal
critical to the higher-coordinate trimodal critical at every attracting point
except one banded-support hole.  An 8192-seed survivor reconstruction closes
that hole prospectively under factor-two RK4 step refinement, complete
attractor-capture controls, and five-return DOP853 audits.  This qualifies a
local operational critical identity.  A separately frozen source-geometric
test then qualifies the operational historical mapping
$K_1\mapsto C$, $K_0\mapsto D$, and
$(B_0,B_1,B_2)\mapsto(2,1,0)$ across DOP853 and Radau, both coordinates,
and held-out return segments.  A first exact-landmark word test then qualifies
the period-6 orbit and step-stable x topology but fails: z is monotone, and the
x words from two fixed steps match none of the frozen period-6 targets.  This
rejects the printed coordinate as a reproducible symbolic center, not the full
conceptual word set.  An independently located superstable center and a global
topology-transition curve remain open.  A target-word-blind raster around the
other exact period-6 landmark then resolves 40,401 points at 50 times the
earlier atlas resolution per parameter axis.  The anchor belongs to an
eight-connected 981-pixel period-6 component over
$a\in[0.2145,0.21555]$.  This component reaches both boundaries of the sampled
$c\in[7.4,7.8]$ interval while remaining interior in $a$.  It supplies a
coherent alternative search band but requires vertical expansion before
raster connectivity, corrected-orbit identity, or critical membership can be
tested.  The prospective 92,736-pixel expansion then reproduces both exact
landmarks as period 6 but places them in distinct eight-connected stable
raster components.  The second landmark's 2,598-pixel component spans
$c\in[7.124,8.192]$, whereas the first landmark's nearest period-6 pixel at
$c=6.124$ is outside it.  This rejects a shared resolved stable raster band,
not a subgrid bridge or continuation through unstable period-6 orbits.
These results do not yet continue a continuous invariant-saddle transition
curve through the parameter plane or establish the proposed global
two/three-branch curve,
third-branch reinjection mechanism, logistic conjugacy, or an explanation of
the entire parameter plane.  We state the experiments required to decide
those remaining claims and preserve their provenance in a reproducible
research record.
\end{abstract}
